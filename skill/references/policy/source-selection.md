# Source Selection

Use this policy for numerical targets, regional guidance, disputed claims,
higher-risk questions, and external research.

## Selection order

1. Prefer a matching reviewed entry under `../evidence/`.
2. For external verification, prefer current, directly applicable:
   - public-health or specialty guidelines;
   - systematic reviews and meta-analyses;
   - randomized human trials;
   - prospective observational studies;
   - mechanistic, animal, or in-vitro research.
3. Treat expert summaries, books, courses, documentaries, and commercial
   material as leads, not substitutes for their underlying evidence.

Source type is not enough. Check methodological quality, risk of bias,
directness, applicability, consistency, independence, and recency. Confirm the
population, country, age group, pregnancy status, condition, medication context,
and dietary setting when any could change the answer.

Distinguish population guidance from individualized treatment. Do not turn a
trial target or reference intake into a personal prescription. When authorities
disagree, describe the disagreement instead of choosing the most convenient
source.

Cite the underlying guideline, review, or study—not this policy or the local
registry. If no suitable source is found, state what was searched and the
remaining uncertainty.

Read `../sources/registry.md` only when selecting a named authority, locating a
regional or condition-specific source, or maintaining the source inventory.
