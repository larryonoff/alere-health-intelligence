# Safe Personal Experiments

Use this workflow when a user wants to test, track, optimize, or adopt part of
a named personal protocol.

## Intervention ladder

Choose the lowest sufficient level:

1. **Foundation** — regular meals, dietary variety, sleep opportunity, ordinary
   movement, social support, and removal of a practical obstacle.
2. **Small behavioral experiment** — one reversible change with a short review
   window.
3. **Qualified care** — symptoms, diagnoses, medication, therapeutic diets,
   laboratory interpretation, or supplement dosing.
4. **Experimental intervention** — do not recommend unlicensed therapies,
   off-label medication, research-only tests, or surrogate biological-age
   optimization.

Do not escalate to a higher level when a lower level can address the user's
ordinary goal.

## Behavioral experiment

1. Define one concrete question, such as whether preparing breakfast in advance
   makes weekday eating easier.
2. Record a simple baseline only when it helps comparison.
3. Choose one reversible action that does not create medical risk or severe
   restriction.
4. Select a short review window appropriate to the behavior, usually several
   days to two weeks.
5. Track the minimum useful observations:
   - whether the action was attempted;
   - effort, cost, and inconvenience;
   - subjective usefulness or well-being;
   - one relevant non-diagnostic observation when needed.
6. Decide to keep, adjust, or stop the action.

Do not change multiple variables and then attribute the result to one of them.
Do not treat wearable data, a single laboratory value, or a biological-age
score as a diagnosis or meaningful health outcome by itself.

## Stop and route

Stop the experiment and recommend appropriate professional care when it causes
significant symptoms, rapid unintended weight change, persistent fatigue,
fainting, disrupted menstruation, allergic symptoms, worsening mental health,
or signs of disordered eating.

Never design personal experiments involving prescription medication,
supplement doses, fasting or material energy restriction, pregnancy, children,
chronic-disease treatment, heat or oxygen therapy, injections, or unlicensed
procedures.

## Named protocols and influencers

Separate:

- the person's reported routine;
- evidence supporting each component;
- uncertainty and applicability;
- commercial interests;
- cost, burden, and opportunity cost.

An N-of-1 result can generate a question but cannot establish general efficacy.
Prefer independent guidelines and human outcome evidence over biomarkers,
testimonials, affiliate links, or products sold by the protocol owner.
