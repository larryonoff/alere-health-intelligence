# Known Failure Modes

Read this for claim verification, conflicting advice, conditions, supplements,
and longevity questions. These are concrete errors seen in practice.

## Evidence and citations

- **Borrowed credibility.** A strong citation for one claim does not support an
  adjacent claim about another population, dose, or outcome.
- **Certainty inflation under pressure.** Repeat the honest evidence assessment
  when the user wants reassurance; do not upgrade it.
- **Surrogate reported as outcome.** A biomarker, epigenetic clock, or risk score
  is not a demonstrated change in health.
- **Mechanism accepted as outcome.** A genuine mechanism does not establish that
  acting on it helps a person.
- **Agreement mistaken for verification.** When an unsourced claim matches the
  reviewed corpus, use the corpus assessment but cite its underlying source. The
  speaker or course is still not the source.
- **Debunking by association.** A claim is not false because an unreliable
  speaker made it or because it is common in wellness marketing. Check the
  literature before accepting or rejecting it.

## Guidance and personal application

- **Trial protocol read as prescription.** A supervised trial target describes
  the intervention tested, not what to prescribe to an individual.
- **Guideline threshold read as personal target.** Population definitions and
  reference values are not individualized targets.
- **Averaging away disagreement.** Describe a genuine disagreement between
  reputable authorities instead of blending their positions.
- **Credential accepted as citation.** Qualification can make a claim plausible
  but does not evidence it. Do not dismiss the person either; evaluate the claim.

## Safety and refusal

- **Optimizing the wrong problem.** Restriction, fastest-possible framing, food
  guilt, or body dissatisfaction requires `disordered-eating-safety.md`, not a
  more optimized meal plan.
- **Corpus gap reported as evidence gap.** Say the local corpus has no reviewed
  answer. Search authoritative sources when possible. Say no reliable source was
  found only after searching, or disclose that verification is unavailable.
- **Asserted absence of harm read as reassurance.** Absolute claims such as “no
  contraindications” or “impossible to overdose” require a safety check.
- **Elimination accepted as diagnosis.** For suspected coeliac disease, removing
  gluten before clinical testing can make diagnosis harder. Read
  `evidence/conditions/coeliac-and-gluten.md` and state the testing boundary
  before discussing elimination.
