## Why retain this card

Blueprint is a useful case study for distinguishing ordinary health habits from
personal optimization, commercial products, surrogate biomarkers, medication,
and experimental procedures. It is not an authoritative protocol for Alere.

## Potentially reusable concepts

Promote only after independent support and independent wording:

- prioritize foundations before expensive optimization;
- reduce decision burden with repeatable systems;
- use one bounded and reversible behavioral experiment;
- evaluate feasibility, cost, and quality of life alongside observations;
- treat tracking as feedback rather than proof of health;
- keep movement, sleep opportunity, nutrition, and social connection visible
  as separate lifestyle domains.

These concepts are already partly represented in `workflows.md` and
`self-experimentation.md`.

## Content not approved for runtime

- personal calorie and macronutrient targets;
- caloric restriction or early meal cutoff as universal longevity advice;
- supplement stacks and doses;
- prescription or off-label medication;
- cycle-phase nutrition or training prescriptions;
- biological-age reversal claims;
- sauna detoxification;
- hyperbaric oxygen, injections, stem cells, gene therapy, or research-only
  testing;
- personal biomarker percentiles as evidence of general efficacy;
- branded products, graphics, recipes, or protocol text.

## Reasons for limitation

- N-of-1 experience with many simultaneous interventions;
- attribution to an individual component is not possible;
- reliance on surrogate and experimental measurements;
- unclear generalizability across sex, age, disease, resources, and culture;
- commercial products and linked services;
- mixture of ordinary, off-label, unlicensed, and research-use interventions;
- noncommercial reuse restriction stated by the site.

## Independent verification queue

- Meal timing: compare with randomized trials and systematic reviews rather
  than the protocol owner's sleep or glucose observations.
- Caloric restriction: distinguish cardiometabolic markers from human
  longevity and clinical outcomes.
- Wearables: assess validation for the exact device and metric; avoid diagnosis.
- Biological-age measures: assess calibration, repeatability, clinical meaning,
  and whether a change predicts outcomes.
- Sauna: separate observational cardiovascular associations, heat safety, and
  unsupported detoxification claims.
- Menstrual-cycle adaptation: require high-quality female-specific evidence and
  avoid universal phase-based prescriptions.

## Editorial decision

Keep Blueprint in `source-catalog.md` as a named limited source. Do not copy its
content into runtime. Create original recipes only from ordinary culinary ideas
and support health claims through independent authorities.
