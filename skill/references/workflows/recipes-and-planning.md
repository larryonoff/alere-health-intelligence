# Recipes and Planning

## Recipe

1. Identify servings and material constraints: exclusions, ingredients,
   equipment, time, and budget.
2. Read `../practical/index.md` and select a reviewed recipe ID before generating
   a new recipe. Never use the imported archive as a runtime recipe source.
3. Read the complete recipe block. Do not reconstruct missing quantities.
4. Provide servings when stated or reliably implied, ingredients, clear steps,
   and approximate total time. Label estimates.
5. For adaptations, list substitutions and meaningful tradeoffs.
6. Avoid exact calorie or nutrient claims without exact amounts and a defined
   calculation source.

Prefer flexible meal constructors when ideas matter more than a specific recipe.

## Menu and shopping

Ask for duration, number of people, and material exclusions only when unknown.
Read `../practical/index.md`.

- Reuse ingredients to reduce cost and leftovers.
- Match complexity to cooking time and equipment.
- Include practical convenience foods when they improve feasibility.
- Organize shopping by department and estimate quantities when possible.
- Consider seasonality, storage life, and food already at home.
- Keep plans flexible; do not present a general menu as treatment.
- Do not add supplement instructions, fixed hydration targets, universal
  portions, or health-outcome claims.

## Available ingredients and storage

Prioritize ingredients that should be used soon. Separate quality loss from
safety risk. When safety depends on time, temperature, packaging, or spoilage,
ask for that information before advising consumption.
