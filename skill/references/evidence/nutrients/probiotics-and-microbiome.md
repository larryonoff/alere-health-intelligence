# Probiotics and the gut microbiome

Read this for questions about probiotic supplements, taking them alongside or after
antibiotics, "good versus bad" bacteria, soil-based organisms, and the fetal or
infant microbiome.

The claims in circulation run to both extremes — probiotics as a cure-all, and
probiotics as always harmful. Both are wrong, and the second has authoritative
evidence against it that is easy to miss because one well-known study points the
other way.

## Claims

### Fibre feeds saccharolytic fermentation and short-chain fatty acid production

**Strength: strong** for the mechanism.

Dietary fibre is the primary substrate for colonic bacterial fermentation, and
butyrate is the main energy source for colonocytes. Diet reshapes microbial
composition within days.

- Flint HJ. The impact of nutrition on the human microbiome. *Proceedings of the
  Nutrition Society* 2015;74(1):13–22.
  DOI: [10.1017/S0029665114001463](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0029665114001463).
- Carlsen H, Pajari AM. Dietary fiber — a scoping review for Nordic Nutrition
  Recommendations 2023. *Food & Nutrition Research* 2023;67.
  DOI: [10.29219/fnr.v67.9979](https://doi.org/10.29219/fnr.v67.9979).

See `fiber.md` for intake figures and outcome evidence. Fibre advice stands on its
own; it does not need a microbiome mechanism to justify it.

### "Good bacteria" versus "bad bacteria" is not how the field describes it

**Strength: moderate.**

There is no universally beneficial or harmful gut bacterium, and no single
definable healthy microbiome — eubiosis is personalised and context-dependent.
Popular metrics such as the Firmicutes-to-Bacteroidetes ratio span a wide range in
healthy people and are not health measures.

More usefully: function is more reproducible than species roster. Short-chain fatty
acid output has been shown to be consistent across donors even when different
species performed the fermentation.

- Shanahan F, Ghosh TS, O'Toole PW. The healthy microbiome — what is the definition
  of a healthy gut microbiome? *Gastroenterology* 2021;160(2):483–494.
  PMID: [33253682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33253682/).
- Reichardt N, Vollmer M, Holtrop G, et al. Specific substrate-driven changes in
  human faecal microbiota composition contrast with functional redundancy in
  short-chain fatty acid production. *ISME Journal* 2018;12(2):610–622.
  PMID: [29192904](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29192904/).

Avoid the good/bad framing when answering. Describe substrate, fermentation, and
diversity instead.

### Probiotics with antibiotics: beneficial for specific indications, not universally

**Strength: moderate.**

This is the claim most often stated backwards. A Cochrane review found probiotics
reduce the risk of *Clostridioides difficile*–associated diarrhoea by about 60%:
**RR 0.40 (95% CI 0.30–0.52)** across 31 trials and 8,672 participants,
approximately one case prevented per 65 people treated, **GRADE moderate
certainty**. Adverse events were not increased — if anything reduced,
RR 0.83 (0.71–0.97) at very low certainty.

- Esmaeilinezhad Z, Kwok C, et al. Probiotics for the prevention of
  *Clostridioides difficile*–associated diarrhea in adults and children. *Cochrane
  Database of Systematic Reviews* 2025.
  DOI: [10.1002/14651858.CD006095.pub5](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006095.pub5).

Guidelines are indication-specific rather than blanket in either direction. The AGA
recommends particular strains for *C. difficile* prevention in adults and children
on antibiotics, for necrotising enterocolitis prevention in preterm low-birthweight
infants, and for pouchitis — while recommending *against* use in Crohn's disease,
ulcerative colitis, and IBS.

- Su GL, Ko CW, Bercik P, et al. AGA clinical practice guidelines on the role of
  probiotics in the management of gastrointestinal disorders. *Gastroenterology*
  2020;159(2):697–705.
  DOI: [10.1053/j.gastro.2020.05.059](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2020.05.059).

### The study showing impaired recolonisation is real, and measures a different outcome

**Strength: moderate.**

An often-cited Israeli study found that after antibiotics, an 11-strain probiotic
produced markedly delayed and persistently incomplete mucosal microbiome recovery
compared with watchful waiting, while autologous faecal transplant restored it
within days. The finding is genuine and correctly describes what was measured.

- Suez J, Zmora N, Zilberman-Schapira G, et al. Post-antibiotic gut mucosal
  microbiome reconstitution is impaired by probiotics and improved by autologous
  FMT. *Cell* 2018;174(6):1406–1423.
  DOI: [10.1016/j.cell.2018.08.047](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2018.08.047).

The two results are compatible, and the distinction is the whole point: Suez
measured **microbiome recolonisation**, a surrogate. Cochrane measured **clinical
diarrhoea**, a patient outcome. A surrogate moving unfavourably does not overturn an
outcome moving favourably — see the failure mode in `../../../SKILL.md` on
surrogates reported as outcomes.

"Probiotics are never needed and that is the official recommendation" is therefore
false on both halves. Say what each study measured rather than picking a side.

### Colonisation is host-determined, and non-colonisation is not a safety argument

**Strength: moderate.**

Probiotic organisms generally do not permanently colonise the gut, and whether they
transiently do is determined by the host, idiosyncratically — not by whether the
person "needs" them.

- Zmora N, Zilberman-Schapira G, Suez J, et al. Personalized gut mucosal
  colonization resistance to empiric probiotics is associated with unique host and
  microbiome features. *Cell* 2018;174(6):1388–1405.
  DOI: [10.1016/j.cell.2018.08.041](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2018.08.041).

The claim that organisms a person does not need "simply will not take hold, so
there is no risk" inverts this. Non-colonisation is near-universal, so it
distinguishes nothing, and harm does not require colonisation — the Suez result
above came from transient organisms.

Product quality is a documented problem: commercial *Bacillus*-based preparations
have been found mislabelled, enterotoxin-producing, or without viable organisms.
There are no FDA-approved probiotics for infants.

- Zhu K, Hölzel CS, Cui Y, et al. Probiotic *Bacillus cereus* strains, a potential
  risk for public health in China. *Frontiers in Microbiology* 2016;7:718.
  PMID: [27242738](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27242738/).

### A healthy fetus is not colonised in utero

**Strength: strong.**

The current best-supported position is the sterile-womb hypothesis. A large
multidisciplinary analysis concluded that reported fetal microbiome signals are
most likely contamination artefacts of low-biomass sequencing.

- Kennedy KM, de Goffau MC, Perez-Muñoz ME, et al. Questioning the fetal microbiome
  illustrates pitfalls of low-biomass microbial studies. *Nature*
  2023;613(7945):639–649.
  DOI: [10.1038/s41586-022-05546-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05546-8).

What *is* supported: the fetus is exposed prenatally to maternally derived
microbial **metabolites** and immune signals that shape immune development.
Exposure to metabolites is not colonisation. "The sterile womb is outdated" states
the field's position backwards.

## What to say

1. Answer probiotic questions by indication and strain, never as a class verdict in
   either direction.
2. When a user cites the impaired-recolonisation study, confirm it is real and name
   what it measured, then give the clinical-outcome evidence alongside it.
3. Drop good/bad bacteria framing; talk about fibre, fermentation, and diversity.
4. If a user is considering a probiotic for an infant, or is immunocompromised,
   critically ill, or has a central line, route to a clinician before anything else.
5. Recommend dietary fibre and variety on their own evidence rather than as a
   microbiome intervention.

## Boundary

- Do not recommend a specific probiotic product, strain, or dose. Naming what
  guidelines cover is not the same as prescribing it.
- Do not advise probiotics for infants, preterm infants, immunocompromised or
  critically ill people, or anyone with a central venous catheter. Route per
  `../../safety-and-context.md`.
- Do not interpret a microbiome test result. Consumer gut-microbiome panels have no
  validated clinical interpretation.
- Do not present probiotics as treatment for IBS, IBD, autoimmune disease, or mood
  disorders.
- Do not attribute an individual's symptoms to dysbiosis.
