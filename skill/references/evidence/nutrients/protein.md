# Protein

The most-asked nutrition question, and now a contested one: the 2025–2030 US
Dietary Guidelines raised protein emphasis substantially, and leading academic
groups dispute it. Read `../guidelines/conflicting-guidance.md` alongside this.

## Claims

### The population reference intake is 0.8 g/kg/day for general adults

**Strength: strong.**

Established reference values across authorities. This is the amount that meets the
requirements of nearly all healthy adults — a population reference point, not an
optimum and not a ceiling.

- EFSA dietary reference values:
  <https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/dietary-reference-values>
- Nordic Nutrition Recommendations 2023: <https://pub.norden.org/nord2023-003/>

Most people in high-income countries already exceed this. Deficiency is rare
outside illness, very low energy intake, or restrictive eating.

### Older adults likely need more than 0.8 g/kg/day

**Strength: moderate.**

The PROT-AGE position paper considers 0.8 g/kg/day too low for older people and
recommends **1.0–1.2 g/kg/day** to maintain and regain muscle. A 2025 study using
indicator amino acid oxidation in older adults with sarcopenia estimated an
average requirement of 1.21 g/kg/day and a recommended intake of 1.54 g/kg/day —
first-of-its-kind, small, and therefore not yet guideline strength.

- Bauer J et al. Evidence-based recommendations for optimal dietary protein intake
  in older people: a position paper from the PROT-AGE Study Group.
  *J Am Med Dir Assoc* 2013;14(8):542–559.
  DOI: [10.1016/j.jamda.2013.05.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamda.2013.05.021).
  PMID: [23867520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23867520/).
- Dietary protein requirements of older adults with sarcopenia determined by the
  indicator amino acid oxidation technique. *Front Nutr* 2025.
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11906324/>

Protein alone does not build or preserve muscle. Resistance exercise is the other
required half; protein without it produces much less benefit.

### Source matters for long-term outcomes

**Strength: moderate.**

A dose-response meta-analysis of prospective cohorts found higher plant protein
intake associated with lower all-cause mortality: an additional 3% of energy from
plant protein per day was associated with a 5% lower risk of all-cause death.

- Naghshi S, Sadeghi O, Willett WC, Esmaillzadeh A. Dietary intake of total,
  animal, and plant proteins and risk of all cause, cardiovascular, and cancer
  mortality: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective
  cohort studies. *BMJ* 2020;370:m2412.
  DOI: [10.1136/bmj.m2412](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m2412).
  PMID: [32699048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32699048/).

Substitution is a separate question and needs a separate source. A systematic
review of isocaloric substitution found replacing animal protein with plant
protein inversely associated with all-cause mortality in 4 of 5 studies
(RR 0.54–0.95) and with cardiovascular mortality in all 4 studies examining it
(RR 0.58–0.91).

- Zheng Z, Zhu W, Yang B, Zhao Y, Li H, Park HY, et al. The isocaloric
  substitution of plant-based and animal-based protein in relation to
  aging-related health outcomes: a systematic review. *Nutrients*
  2022;14(2):272.
  DOI: [10.3390/nu14020272](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu14020272).

Observational, so confounding cannot be excluded — hence `moderate`.

### For muscle mass specifically, animal protein performs somewhat better

**Strength: moderate.**

Meta-analyses of randomized trials found plant protein produced lower muscle mass
gain than animal protein, with stronger differences in adults under 60 than in
those 60 and over.

- Effect of plant versus animal protein on muscle mass, strength, physical
  performance, and sarcopenia: systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs. 2025.
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12166177/>
- Animal protein versus plant protein in supporting lean mass and muscle strength:
  systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs. 2021.
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7926405/>

**This is the honest tension in the topic** and it must not be resolved by
picking the convenient side: plant protein looks better for long-term mortality
associations, animal protein somewhat better for short-term muscle outcomes. The
differences are modest, adequate total intake and resistance training matter more
than source, and plant sources can close the muscle gap with sufficient quantity
and variety.

### The 2025–2030 US guideline recommendation is disputed

**Strength: disputed.**

The DGA 2025–2030 recommends substantially higher protein without distinguishing
sources. Harvard's Nutrition Source and Stanford's nutrition group both note that
most Americans already meet protein needs, that fibre is underconsumed by
comparison, and that plant protein and fish are associated with more favourable
outcomes than diets high in red meat.

See `../guidelines/conflicting-guidance.md` for how to present this.

## Boundary

- **Never set a personal gram target** for anyone with chronic kidney disease,
  reduced kidney function, liver disease, or on protein-restricted medical
  nutrition therapy. Higher protein intake is contraindicated in some kidney
  conditions and this is a clinical decision.
- Do not convert a population reference value or a trial figure into an individual
  prescription. Explain the reference range and what it is based on.
- Protein supplements and powders are supplement-dosing questions — route per
  `../../safety-and-context.md`.
- Route pregnancy, breastfeeding, childhood, diagnosed sarcopenia, and
  unintentional weight loss.
- Very high protein intake framed as body optimization can accompany disordered
  eating; check `../../disordered-eating-safety.md` when the request has that
  shape.

## How to use this entry

Two useful corrections, depending on which way the person is wrong:

- Worried about deficiency → most people already meet requirements; the question
  is usually distribution and source, not total.
- Chasing very high intake → the marginal benefit is small without resistance
  training, fibre is more likely to be the actual gap, and source has long-term
  consequences.
