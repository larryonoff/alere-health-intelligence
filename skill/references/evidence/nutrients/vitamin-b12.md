# Vitamin B12

Read this whenever B12 comes up: a user on a plant-based diet, someone told they do
not need it, someone asking about dose, or someone quoting that overdose is
impossible.

Two claims in circulation matter more than the rest. "Gut bacteria make enough B12,
so supplements are unnecessary" can cause irreversible neurological injury and is
the highest-harm nutrition claim in this corpus. "It is water-soluble so overdose is
physically impossible" is used to justify unlimited dosing and misrepresents an
evidence gap as an all-clear.

## Claims

### A reliable B12 source is non-negotiable on a diet without animal products

**Strength: strong.**

EFSA sets an adequate intake of **4 µg/day for adults**, 4.5 µg/day in pregnancy and
5 µg/day in lactation. That figure is not reachable from unfortified plant foods.
Supplements or fortified foods are required — this is not a matter of optimisation.

- EFSA NDA Panel. Scientific opinion on dietary reference values for cobalamin
  (vitamin B12). *EFSA Journal* 2015;13(7):4150.
  DOI: [10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4150](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4150).

Deficiency causes megaloblastic anaemia and subacute combined degeneration of the
spinal cord. Neurological damage can be **irreversible** if deficiency is prolonged,
and it can develop before anaemia appears — so a normal blood count does not exclude
it. This asymmetry is why the claim below is dangerous rather than merely wrong.

### Colonic bacterial synthesis does not meet requirements

**Strength: strong.**

Bacteria in the human colon do produce cobalamin, but they do so **distal to the
ileum**, where intrinsic-factor-mediated absorption occurs. Production at that site
cannot be absorbed in meaningful quantity. The claim that eating enough fresh plant
food makes gut synthesis sufficient has no support, and acting on it risks permanent
neurological harm.

Related claims that are also unsupported: that unwashed produce, soil residue,
spirulina, chlorella, nutritional yeast (unless fortified), tempeh, or other
fermented foods supply adequate B12. Analogues in some algae are not
bioavailable cobalamin and can register on assays without providing activity.

### Standard dosing, and why oral doses are large

**Strength: moderate.**

Intrinsic-factor-mediated absorption saturates at roughly 1.5–2 µg per dose, so
fractional absorption falls sharply as dose rises — approximately 50% at doses of
about 1 µg or below, dropping toward 0.5% at 1000 µg. Large oral doses are therefore
the standard approach, and passive diffusion carries the remainder. The mechanism
behind "take a big dose" is real.

Commonly advised regimens: fortified foods two to three times daily totalling at
least 3 µg; **or a daily supplement of at least 10 µg; or 2000 µg once weekly**.

- Vegan Society. What every vegan should know about vitamin B12.
  <https://www.vegansociety.com/resources/nutrition-and-health/nutrients/vitamin-b12/what-every-vegan-should-know-about-vitamin-b12>

Doses in the hundreds of micrograms daily are well above these regimens without a
stated rationale. They are not established as harmful, but they are pharmacological
rather than nutritional, and the standard options above are what guidance supports.
Do not recommend a specific dose to an individual — state what the regimens are and
route dosing decisions to a clinician.

### "No upper limit" means insufficient data, not proven safety

**Strength: moderate.**

EFSA has **not set a tolerable upper intake level** for B12 — because the data are
inadequate to derive one, not because harm has been excluded. Water solubility is
not a general safety argument for any nutrient.

There is randomised evidence of harm from B12 given **with folic acid**, in a
population that is not the one usually asking. Pooled analysis of two double-blind
placebo-controlled trials, 6837 patients with ischaemic heart disease receiving folic
acid 0.8 mg/day plus B12 0.4 mg/day:

- cancer incidence **HR 1.21 (95% CI 1.03–1.41, P = .02)**;
- cancer mortality **HR 1.38 (1.07–1.79, P = .01)**;
- all-cause mortality **HR 1.18 (1.04–1.33, P = .01)**.

- Ebbing M, Bønaa KH, Nygård O, et al. Cancer incidence and mortality after
  treatment with folic acid and vitamin B12. *JAMA* 2009;302(19):2119–2126.
  PMID: [19920236](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19920236/).

State the limits of this honestly. It was a **combination** intervention, so B12
alone is not implicated and folic acid is the more suspected agent; the population
was cardiac patients in a country without folic-acid fortification, not healthy
people on plant-based diets. It does not show that B12 supplementation harms vegans.
It does refute the categorical claim that no adverse effect is possible.

### Elevated serum B12 is usually a marker, not a cause

**Strength: moderate.**

Observational associations between high serum B12 and mortality are substantially
confounded by reverse causation: elevated levels flag liver and kidney disease and
some malignancies. Associations have attenuated or disappeared after adjustment for
liver function. Do not present these cohort findings as evidence that
supplementation is dangerous — that would be the mirror-image error of the claim
above.

## What to say

1. Lead with the requirement: a reliable source is mandatory, not optional. If the
   user has been told otherwise, correct it before discussing anything else.
2. If they cite gut synthesis, unwashed vegetables, spirulina, or fermented foods,
   name why each fails rather than dismissing the topic.
3. Give the standard regimens as options; do not prescribe a dose for the individual.
4. If asked whether overdose is possible, say no upper limit has been set **because
   data are insufficient**, and do not endorse unlimited intake.
5. Route to a clinician for testing where deficiency is plausible — long-term
   plant-based diet without supplementation, metformin or PPI use, gastric or ileal
   surgery, age over 60, pregnancy, or any neurological symptom.

## Boundary

- Do not diagnose deficiency, interpret serum B12, holotranscobalamin,
  methylmalonic acid, or homocysteine results.
- Do not recommend injections, or advise on switching between oral and parenteral
  routes. Pernicious anaemia — intrinsic factor autoimmunity — requires clinical
  management, and the claim that injections are needed only after bowel resection is
  wrong.
- Do not give paediatric or infant doses. Maternal intake governs infant status in
  breastfed infants; route per `../../safety-and-context.md`.
- Do not advise stopping or reducing a clinician-prescribed regimen.
- Any neurological symptom — numbness, paraesthesia, gait or cognitive change — is
  urgent clinical territory, not a nutrition conversation.
