# Intestinal permeability, zonulin, and "leaky gut"

Read this when a user asks about leaky gut, intestinal permeability, zonulin, or
a claim that gut barrier dysfunction causes autoimmune disease.

This topic is easy to get wrong in **both** directions. The barrier mechanism is
real, published, and mechanistically specific. The consumer-facing syndrome, its
tests, and its cure claims are not supported. Dismissing the whole subject as
pseudoscience is as inaccurate as endorsing the protocols sold around it.

## Claims

### The barrier and zonulin mechanism

**Strength: moderate.**

The intestinal epithelium is held together by tight junctions that regulate what
crosses into circulation. Zonulin (pre-haptoglobin-2) is a physiologic modulator
of those junctions. In genetically susceptible individuals, deregulation of the
zonulin pathway is implicated in the development of specific autoimmune and
inflammatory conditions, with coeliac disease and type 1 diabetes the
best-characterised examples.

- Fasano A. All disease begins in the (leaky) gut: role of zonulin-mediated gut
  permeability in the pathogenesis of some chronic inflammatory diseases.
  *F1000Research* 2020;9:F1000 Faculty Rev-69.
  PMID: [32051759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32051759/).
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6996528/>

The strongest directional evidence is interventional rather than correlational.
In lupus models, *Ruminococcus gnavus* strains from patients induced
zonulin-mediated permeability and autoimmune features, and permeability was
reversed by larazotide acetate, a specific zonulin antagonist.

- Sex-dependent lupus *Blautia (Ruminococcus) gnavus* strain induction of
  zonulin-mediated intestinal permeability and autoimmunity.
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9405438/>

Note what this supports: a mechanism operating in particular conditions. It does
not establish that permeability is modifiable by diet in a given patient, nor
that modifying it treats an established disease.

### "Leaky gut syndrome" as a diagnosis

**Strength: insufficient.**

Leaky gut syndrome is not an accepted formal medical diagnosis, and no validated
test establishes it. Blood tests for it do not exist; commercial stool panels are
unregulated with minimal supporting evidence. Even objective permeability
measurement lacks standardised interpretation, and increased permeability is not
invariably harmful — its relationship to symptoms is unclear.

- Lacy BE, Wise JL, Cangemi DJ. Leaky Gut Syndrome: Myths and Management.
  *Gastroenterol Hepatol (N Y)* 2024;20(5).
  <https://www.gastroenterologyandhepatology.net/archives/may-2024/leaky-gut-syndrome-myths-and-management/>

### Single-root-cause claims

**Strength: insufficient.**

Zonulin is not implicated in all chronic inflammatory disease, and not all such
disease involves increased permeability. For several conditions — Crohn's disease
and coeliac disease among them — altered permeability appears to be a consequence
of the disease process rather than its origin.

- GI Society (Canada), leaky gut syndrome:
  <https://badgut.org/information-centre/a-z-digestive-topics/leaky-gut-syndrome/>

"Permeability is the root cause of all autoimmune disease, close it and everything
resolves" is therefore not supported, even though the underlying mechanism is.
The direction of causation is genuinely unsettled and differs by condition.

### Commercial zonulin testing

**Strength: moderate** for the topic; the assay-invalidity finding itself is
well replicated. Labelled `moderate` so the entry never reads as strong evidence
*about permeability* — see the note under Frontmatter below.

Widely sold zonulin ELISA kits do not measure zonulin. Antibody-capture, mass
spectrometry, and Western blot work showed the kits fail to detect recombinant
pre-haptoglobin-2 and instead recognise other serum proteins, properdin being the
leading candidate. Reported "zonulin" values also do not track haptoglobin
genotype.

- Scheffler L, Crane A, Heyne H, et al. Widely Used Commercial ELISA Does Not
  Detect Precursor of Haptoglobin2, but Recognizes Properdin as a Potential
  Second Member of the Zonulin Family. *Front Endocrinol* 2018;9:22.
  doi:10.3389/fendo.2018.00022 — PMID 29459849.
  <https://doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2018.00022>
- Serum zonulin as a marker of intestinal mucosal barrier function: may not be
  what it seems. *PLOS One* 2019.
  <https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0210728>

A research-grade mechanism and a purchasable test are different things. This
distinction is usually what matters practically to the user.

## What to say

1. The mechanism is real research — say so plainly. Do not call it a myth.
2. Name the boundary: established for specific conditions, not a universal cause,
   and not a treatment target a user can act on alone.
3. If the user wants a test, state the assay problem and that no validated
   clinical test exists. Do not name a laboratory or panel.
4. For an established autoimmune diagnosis, the treating specialist governs. Never
   suggest dietary change substitutes for or permits reducing therapy.
5. Fibre diversity and dietary pattern advice stands on its own evidence — see
   `fiber.md` and `../longevity/dietary-patterns.md`. Do not justify it through a
   permeability mechanism; it does not need one.

## Boundary

- Do not offer permeability-directed elimination protocols, "gut healing"
  regimens, or supplement stacks.
- Do not interpret a zonulin result the user already has beyond stating the assay
  limitation.
- Do not connect permeability to a specific autoimmune diagnosis for an individual.
  Route per `../../safety-and-context.md`.
- Larazotide is an investigational drug, not a nutrition recommendation. Never
  present it as available treatment.
