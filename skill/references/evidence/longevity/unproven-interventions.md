# Longevity interventions without established human benefit

Read this whenever a user asks about a longevity supplement, drug, fasting
protocol, or biological-age test. Most of what the longevity market sells sits
here.

This file exists because saying "no reliable human evidence" accurately is a
service, not a refusal. It is also the clearest demonstration of what
strength-of-evidence labeling is for.

## Supplements

### NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR)

**Strength: insufficient** for aging outcomes.

These raise blood NAD+ levels in humans — that much is measurable. Meaningful
effects on aging-related outcomes remain unproven. Trials to date are short, small,
and use surrogate endpoints.

- Can supplements containing NMN, NAD+ and resveratrol really slow ageing?
  *The Conversation*, 2026.
  <https://theconversation.com/can-supplements-containing-nmn-nad-and-resveratrol-really-slow-ageing-heres-what-the-evidence-says-282524>

Note also that NMN's regulatory status as a supplement has been contested in the
US, which is a separate matter from efficacy.

### Resveratrol

**Strength: insufficient.**

Poor oral bioavailability. Meta-analyses show no consistent benefit for
cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cancer, or cognitive decline in humans, despite
extensive preclinical work.

The resveratrol story is the canonical example of mechanism and animal data
failing to translate. Useful to cite when explaining why mechanism is not
evidence.

### General pattern for the supplement category

**Strength: insufficient** across the category.

Most anti-aging products are sold on theoretical mechanism and animal data. Where
human trials exist, effects are typically modest or absent. Apply
`../../evidence-strength.md` rule 5: mechanism is not evidence.

## Drugs

### Metformin for longevity in people without diabetes

**Strength: insufficient.**

The TAME trial (Targeting Aging with Metformin) has been delayed since 2016 and
results remain years away. Metformin's benefits in type 2 diabetes are established;
its use as a longevity intervention in people without diabetes is not.

- American Federation for Aging Research. TAME trial. <https://www.afar.org/tame-trial>

Prescription drug — route to a clinician, never advise.

### Rapamycin / sirolimus

**Strength: insufficient** for human longevity, and it carries real risk.

Extends lifespan in model organisms. In humans it is an immunosuppressant, and
researchers remain cautious about preventive use. Off-label longevity use is
outside scope entirely.

Never discuss dosing, sourcing, or protocols. This falls under level 4
(experimental intervention) of `../../self-experimentation.md`, which the skill
does not recommend.

## Energy restriction and fasting

### Caloric restriction in humans

**Strength: weak** for biomarker change; **insufficient** for lifespan.

CALERIE demonstrated that sustained modest caloric restriction is achievable in
healthy adults and produced changes in some biomarkers. It did not demonstrate
extended lifespan, and adverse effects including bone density and lean mass
changes were observed.

- Waziry R et al. Effect of long-term caloric restriction on DNA methylation
  measures of biological aging in healthy adults from the CALERIE trial.
  *Nat Aging* 2023;3:248–257.
  DOI: [10.1038/s43587-022-00357-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-022-00357-y).
  PMID: [37118425](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37118425/).
- National Institute on Aging. Calorie restriction and fasting diets: what do we
  know?
  <https://www.nia.nih.gov/news/calorie-restriction-and-fasting-diets-what-do-we-know>

### Intermittent fasting

**Strength: moderate** for modest weight change; **insufficient** for longevity.

A 2025 BMJ network meta-analysis compared intermittent fasting strategies with
continuous energy restriction. Differences were small and of uncertain clinical
importance; certainty of evidence was limited.

- Semnani-Azad Z et al. Intermittent fasting strategies and their effects on body
  weight and cardiometabolic risk factors: systematic review and network
  meta-analysis of randomised clinical trials. *BMJ* 2025;389:e082007.
  DOI: [10.1136/bmj-2024-082007](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-082007).
  <https://www.bmj.com/content/389/bmj-2024-082007>

Honest summary: fasting is one way to reduce energy intake, with roughly
comparable results to other ways. It is not independently established as a
longevity intervention.

**Never** design a fasting protocol for someone with diabetes on
glucose-lowering medication, a history of disordered eating, pregnancy, or
underweight. Route per `../../safety-and-context.md`.

## Biological age tests

**Strength: disputed.**

Epigenetic clocks estimate age from DNA methylation and predict some outcomes
better than chronological age. But 2026 produced the field's strongest validation
and its most substantive challenge in the same period:

- different published clocks give inconsistent results on the same samples, some
  implying zero or negative "rejuvenation";
- test–retest reliability remains imperfect even after principal-component
  refinements to DunedinPACE and revisions to GrimAge;
- it is unclear how much variance reflects health change versus normal epigenetic
  dynamics and assay noise.

- Biological age clocks: validated and questioned in the same quarter. 2026.
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3050632826000211>
- Epistemic uncertainty challenges aging clock reliability in predicting
  rejuvenation effects.
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11561706/>
- Do we actually need aging clocks? *npj Aging* 2025.
  <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-025-00312-2>

**Do not** treat a biological-age result as a diagnosis, a health outcome, or a
target to optimize. `../../self-experimentation.md` bars surrogate
biological-age optimization outright.

## Named personal protocols

Individual protocols combining many simultaneous interventions cannot establish
causality for any single component, regardless of how thoroughly the individual is
monitored. See `../../editorial-blueprint-protocol.md` and the limited-sources
section of `../../source-catalog.md`.

Separate, always: the person's reported routine; evidence for each component;
uncertainty and applicability; commercial interest; and cost and burden.

## Boundary

- **Prescription drugs are never advised here.** Metformin and rapamycin are
  clinician decisions. Do not discuss dosing, sourcing, off-label use, or
  monitoring.
- Do not set supplement doses, including for compounds with a benign safety
  profile. Dosing is outside scope per `../../safety-and-context.md`.
- Do not design fasting or energy-restriction protocols. Never for someone with
  diabetes on glucose-lowering medication, a history of disordered eating,
  pregnancy or breastfeeding, underweight, or in childhood or adolescence.
- Do not interpret a biological-age result, an epigenetic clock report, or a
  longevity laboratory panel.
- Do not present a surrogate marker change as a health outcome.
- Route symptoms, existing conditions, medication interactions, and any request
  to modify treatment per `../../safety-and-context.md`. Experimental
  interventions are level 4 in `../../self-experimentation.md` and are not
  recommended.

## How to answer

The pattern that works:

1. Name what is actually established — usually "it changes a marker."
2. Name what is not — usually "whether that improves health or lifespan."
3. Note cost, burden, and opportunity cost.
4. Point to what does have evidence — `dietary-patterns.md`, physical activity,
   sleep, not smoking.

This converts a disappointing answer into a useful one. A person asking about NMN
is asking a real question about their healthspan; the honest answer redirects
effort toward what works rather than dismissing the concern.

`../../safety-and-context.md`.
