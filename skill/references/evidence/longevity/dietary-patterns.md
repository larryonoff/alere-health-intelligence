# Dietary patterns, healthspan, and mortality

What the evidence supports about eating for long-term health. Read
`unproven-interventions.md` alongside this — the two together are the honest
picture, and the second one is what distinguishes this corpus from marketing.

## The central limitation, stated first

Almost all diet-and-longevity evidence is **observational**. Randomizing people
to a dietary pattern for decades and measuring death is largely infeasible.
Cohort studies adjust for confounders but cannot eliminate them: people who eat
well also tend to smoke less, move more, and have more money.

Consequence: the strongest honest label for most claims in this file is
`moderate`, and effect sizes are associations, not guaranteed personal outcomes.
Anyone stating otherwise — including a longevity influencer citing these same
studies — is overreaching.

## Claims

### Higher diet quality is associated with lower all-cause mortality

**Strength: moderate.**

Two nationwide cohorts reported all-cause mortality hazard ratios, highest versus
lowest tertile of adherence:

| Index | HR |
| --- | --- |
| EAT-Lancet diet index | 0.87 |
| Overall plant-based diet index | 0.88 |
| Healthful plant-based diet index | 0.90 |

- Associations of adherence to the EAT-Lancet and plant-based diets with mortality
  and life expectancy: two nationwide cohort studies. *GeroScience* 2026.
  DOI: [10.1007/s11357-026-02189-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-026-02189-9).

Interpretation: a hazard ratio of 0.87 is a meaningful population-level
association, not a promise of added years for an individual.

### Mediterranean and DASH patterns have the most consistent support

**Strength: moderate**, with one `strong` component.

These patterns are consistently associated with reduced cardiovascular and
all-cause mortality across cohorts. The cardiovascular component is stronger than
the mortality component because it has randomized trial support.

- Estruch R et al. Primary prevention of cardiovascular disease with a
  Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra-virgin olive oil or nuts (PREDIMED).
  *N Engl J Med* 2018;378(25):e34.
  DOI: [10.1056/NEJMoa1800389](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1800389).
  PMID: [29897866](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29897866/).
- Nordic Nutrition Recommendations 2023. <https://pub.norden.org/nord2023-003/>
- Dietary Patterns for Health-Span and Longevity: A Comprehensive Review.
  *Applied Sciences* 2025;15(22):12013. <https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/22/12013>

PREDIMED is the key randomized evidence, with a cardiovascular endpoint. Note the
trial was retracted and republished after protocol deviations were identified in
part of the cohort; the republished analysis maintained the primary finding.
Mention this if the trial is cited as decisive.

### Diet quality direction matters more than the plant-based label

**Strength: moderate.**

A healthful plant-based pattern is associated with **lower** frailty risk; an
unhealthful plant-based pattern with **higher** risk. Refined grains, sugary
drinks, and processed plant foods are plant-based.

- Association between the quality of plant-based diets and risk of frailty. 2022.
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9745455/>
- Plant-based diets and total and cause-specific mortality: a meta-analysis of
  prospective studies. *Front Nutr* 2025.
  <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1518519/full>

This is the single most useful correction to make when someone asks about
plant-based eating for longevity.

### Diet quality is associated with slower biological ageing measures

**Strength: weak.**

Adherence to DASH, alternative Mediterranean, and HEI-2015 patterns has been
associated with slower ageing as measured by biological ageing metrics and plasma
metabolomic signatures.

- EAT-Lancet and plant-based diets, plasma metabolomic signatures, and biological
  aging. *npj Aging* 2025.
  <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-025-00306-0>

Labeled `weak` deliberately: the outcome is a **surrogate**, not health or
survival, and the ageing measures themselves have contested reliability
(`unproven-interventions.md`). Do not present a biological-age change as a health
outcome.

### Protein adequacy matters for older adults

**Strength: moderate.**

Maintaining muscle mass and function is central to healthspan, and protein
adequacy combined with resistance exercise supports it. Population reference
values are set by EFSA and NNR 2023; requirements for older adults are commonly
considered higher than the general adult reference.

- EFSA dietary reference values.
  <https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/dietary-reference-values>
- Nordic Nutrition Recommendations 2023. <https://pub.norden.org/nord2023-003/>

Do not convert population reference intakes into a personal gram target for
someone with kidney disease or another condition affecting protein handling.

## Boundary

- These are population associations. Never translate a hazard ratio into predicted
  years of life for an individual.
- Healthspan is not a diagnosis and has no clinical measurement. Do not imply it
  can be scored.
- Physical activity, sleep, and not smoking have at least as much support as diet
  for healthspan; nutrition is one domain, not the whole answer. See
  `source-catalog.md` for the ACLM domains and WHO activity guidance.
- Energy restriction, fasting protocols, and supplement regimens belong to
  `unproven-interventions.md` and `../../self-experimentation.md`.

## How to use this entry

The honest and useful message: the dietary patterns with the best longevity
evidence are the same ones with the best evidence for blood pressure, LDL, and
glucose — which is why the condition entries and this file converge. That
convergence is the reason to act, and it is more persuasive than any single
longevity claim.
